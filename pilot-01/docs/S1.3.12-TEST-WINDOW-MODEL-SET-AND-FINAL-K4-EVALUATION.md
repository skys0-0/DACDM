## 3. Why incomplete T1/T2/T3 coverage does not prevent the K4 decision

A full confirmatory execution would require adequate tier coverage, including T3, before K1–K3 could be evaluated. S1.3.12 does not claim otherwise.

K4 is different because the frozen contamination rule is global and monotone:

> a task is excluded if its release date precedes the training cutoff of **any** model used in the test window.

Under this ANY-model rule, adding another model later cannot turn a task already excluded by one selected model into an eligible task. Model-set expansion can only preserve or increase the excluded set.

Therefore, if the frozen anchor set already places the strict known contamination-exclusion fraction above 30%, adding T3 or other providers cannot rescue K4. Full tier expansion becomes unnecessary for the pre-inference termination decision.

## 4. Treatment of contamination indeterminacy

S1.3.11 identified month-precision cutoff boundary cases. S1.3.12 does not force them into the exclusion numerator.

The final K4 decision uses bounds:

- **strict known exclusion fraction** = known `EXCLUDED` / denominator;
- **conservative upper fraction** = (`EXCLUDED` + `INDETERMINATE`) / denominator.

Decision rule:

- strict known fraction > 30% → `K4_TRIGGERED`;
- conservative upper fraction <= 30% → `K4_NOT_TRIGGERED`;
- otherwise → `K4_UNRESOLVED_CONTAMINATION_INDETERMINATE`.

This preserves month-precision uncertainty without allowing uncertainty to hide a K4 trigger already established by known exclusions.
